# SÊRAT CÊNTHINI (SULUK TAMBANGRARAS)

**Composed by Kangjêng Gusti Pangeran Adipati Anom Amêngkunagara III (His Majesty Pakubuwana V) in Surakarta**

## Preface
This Latin-script version of *Sêrat Cênthini* is originally written in Javanese script, consisting of 12 volumes and totaling more than 3,500 pages. The original manuscript is kept in the Sanapustaka of the Surakarta Palace. Meanwhile, those found in the Rêksapustaka Mangkunagaran, the Radya-pustaka library of Sriwêdari, the Museum of Culture in Yogyakarta, the Gêdhong Gajah Museum in Jakarta, and others are all copies or descendants of copies.

To this day, the complete 12 volumes of *Sêrat Cênthini* have never been published or printed in either Javanese or Latin script. In 1912 AD, there was a Latin *Sêrat Cênthini* published by the ‘Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen’, printed by Firma Ruygrok & Co in Batavia, consisting of 4 books containing 8 volumes (volumes 1 to 8), but that Latin print was actually an 'excerpt' from the original volumes 5 to 9. Thus, it is an excerpt from the middle and cannot be called complete.

To be clear:
* Original volumes 1 to 4 and volumes 10 to 12 were not published.
* Details regarding the aforementioned excerpt of *Sêrat Cênthini* will be provided later.

### The Origin of Sêrat Cênthini
The creation of *Sêrat Cênthini* was due to the will of Kangjêng Gusti Pangeran Adipati Anom Amêngkunagara III, son of His Majesty Pakubuwana IV (reigned 1788-1820 AD) in Surakarta. The aforementioned Prince later ascended as Sunuhun Pakubuwana V (1820-1823 AD), who was also known as Sunan Sugih.

The person appointed to lead the composition of *Sêrat Cênthini* was the chief scribe of the principality named Sutrasna, also known as Ki Ngabèi Ranggasutrasna, accompanied by:
1. Radèn Ngabèi Yasadipura II (Radèn Tumênggung Sastranagara), a regent-poet of the principality.
2. Radèn Ngabèi Sastradipura, a chief scribe of the principality.

After performing the Hajj pilgrimage, he changed his name to Kyai Haji Mohammad Ilhar; he was also given assistants:
1. Pangeran Jungut Mandurarêja, a royal relative from Wangga, Klaten, Surakarta.
2. Kyai Kasan Bêsari, a great scholar in Gêbangtinatar, Panaraga, a former servant of Sunuhun Pakubuwana IV.
3. Kyai Mohamad Minhad, a great scholar in Surakarta.

As for the central figure of the story of Jayèngrêsmi's journey, it is Sèh Amongraga. Prince Adipati Anom provided the manuscript ‘Suluk Jatiswara’ and ordered it to be expanded and elaborated brilliantly. Regarding ‘Suluk Jawiswara’, according to the chronogram (*candrasangkala*) of its writing ‘Jati tunggal swara raja’ (1711 Javanese year), this was during the time of His Majesty Pakubuwana III; thus, there is an assumption that the manuscript belonged to PB III.

Many experts were ordered to contribute according to their own fields of knowledge, including Kangjêng Pangulu Tafsiranom regarding the Islamic religion. Other experts wrote about: religion, mysticism (*kabatosan*), the science of ultimate truth/perfection, victory and power, physical prowess (*kanuragan*), medicine, sexual knowledge, primbon calculations, supernatural abilities (*iladuni*), pawukon, the agricultural calendar (*pranatamangsa*), knowledge of offerings, procedures, visiting ancestral graves, ancient sites, remnants of the past, literature, folktales, chronicles, kris (daggers) and their characteristics, horses, birds, architecture, traditional music (*karawitan*), songs (*gêndhing*), dance, planting, agriculture, herbal medicine, and many others, including jokes up to matters of sorrow.

Everything was discussed extensively, deeply, and interestingly, regarding the conditions in mountains, caves, coasts, and similar reports from the coastal regents. Envoys verified sacred and haunted sites and were ordered to make notes as a basis for the composition. Furthermore, the Prince Adipati Anom also personally summarized, edited, and researched all the contents.

The writing of the manuscript began on Saturday Paing, 26th of Sura, with the chronogram 'Paksa suci sabda ji', Javanese year 1742 or 1814 AD. Thus, its age is now 171 years. Once completed, the manuscript was given the name 'Suluk Tambangraras', which is commonly known as '*Sêrat Cênthini*'. The name 'Cênthini' was taken from the name of the servant girl of Nikèn Tambangraras, who became the wife of Sèh Amongraga. Due to the comprehensiveness and vastness of its content, *Sêrat Cênthini* may be called an 'Encyclopedia of Javanese Culture'.

### The 'Excerpt' Sêrat Cênthini
As mentioned previously, the Latin *Sêrat Cênthini* published in Batavia in 1912 was an 'excerpt', and therefore not as complete as the original. The reason was that Sunuhun Pakubuwana VII (1830-1855 AD) ordered the copying of the original *Sêrat Cênthini* from volume 5 to volume 9. In volume 5, it was ordered to add a 'foreword' by the poet Radèn Ngabèi Ranggawarsita. The foreword consists of 17 stanzas of Dhandhanggula and one stanza of Sinom, with the chronogram 'Tata rêsi mulang janma', Javanese year 1785.

The 'excerpt' *Sêrat Cênthini* with the 'foreword' was then presented to the King of the Netherlands. Until now, it is kept at Leiden University in the Netherlands. The excerpt manuscript was then copied by the 'Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen'. Then it was published in Latin script in Batavia. Before printing, it was researched by Radèn Ngabèi Suradipura, Radèn Prawirasuwignya, and Radèn Wirawangsa, and provided with 'Explanatory Notes as a Foreword' by Radèn Mas Arya Surya Suparta, a Javanese translator in Surakarta who later became Kangjêng Gusti Pangeran Adipati Arya Mangkunagara VII (1916-1944) in Surakarta.

It is necessary to publish in Latin script *Sêrat Cênthini*, which is 171 years old and contains vast and comprehensive knowledge of Javanese wisdom and culture, as it is a heritage of the Javanese ancestors that can be called sublime, for this manuscript is the source of Javanese culture. Many Javanese manuscripts quote the contents of *Sêrat Cênthini* but do not mention the source of the quotation. Therefore, *Sêrat Cênthini* urgently needs to be studied and understood by lovers and seekers of knowledge, so that it can eventually become a resource and contribute to the National Culture of Indonesia.

Lovers and seekers can only study *Sêrat Cênthini* if the manuscript is printed and published in Latin script, because there are no Javanese script prints, nor is there a complete Latin one. The one previously published, such as the 1912 edition, is no longer available for sale, and its spelling differs from today's spelling. For this reason, the Latin *Sêrat Cênthini* is greatly desired to be published.

### Efforts by the ‘Cênthini Foundation’
The organization striving for the publication of *Sêrat Cênthini* is the ‘Yayasan Cênthini’ (Yasni) in Yogyakarta, a body specifically working on the Latin *Sêrat Cênthini* until completion, and hopefully, in the future, can publish *Sêrat Cênthini* in the Indonesian language.

It should be known that Yasni is continuing the initiatives of ‘UP Indonesia’ in Yogyakarta, which since 1975 has published:
1. *Pustaka Centhini: A Glance* by Ki Sumidi Adisasmita.
2. *Pusaka Centhini: A Summary of All Contents* by Ki Sumidi Adisasmita.
3. *Serat Centhini Latin* according to the original, volumes 1 to 6.
4. *Serat Centhini told in Indonesian* by Tardjan Hadidjaja and Kamajaya, volumes I-A and I-B.

The Latin *Sêrat Cênthini* mentioned in number 3 above consists of fragments of the original manuscript, i.e.: Original volume 1 became 4 volumes; volumes 5 and 6 are part of original volume 2. Meanwhile, ‘Serat Centhini told in the Indonesian language I-A and I-B’ is an ‘adaptation’ of original volume 1.

Because UP Indonesia ran out of funds and could not continue its intention, several lovers of Javanese Culture, including the main supporter the late Mr. H. Adam Malik, then established the ‘Yayasan Centhini’ (Yasni) specifically to process the Latin *Sêrat Cênthini*, hopefully reaching a publication in the Indonesian language. UP Indonesia has handed over the Latin *Sêrat Cênthini* manuscript of 12 volumes, totaling 5,138 typed folio pages, based on the Javanese manuscript owned by Kangjêng Radèn Mas Arya Suryasurarsa in Mangkunagaran Surakarta, totaling 3,216 folio pages. The source manuscript is kept at the Center for History and Traditional Values Study, Directorate General of Culture, Department of Education and Culture, Yogyakarta.

The cost of publishing the Latin *Sêrat Cênthini* until completion is certainly not small, but Yasni is able to meet it. Initially, Yasni received assistance from the Ford Foundation Indonesia branch in Jakarta, headquartered in the United States. Although the contribution was not enough to publish the Latin *Sêrat Cênthini* until the end, the assistance was of great benefit, as it served as the starting point for the publication of the initial volumes, and hopefully, subsequent volumes can be funded by the sales of those initial volumes.

Therefore, the Centhini Foundation expresses its profound gratitude to the Ford Foundation and all those who have supported the publication of this heritage of the Javanese ancestors, including the assistants in transliterating *Sêrat Cênthini* as well as the lovers and readers who have provided the 'water of life'. May this goal be blessed by God Almighty. Amen.

Yogyakarta, May 20, 1985.

## I. Introduction
Kangjêng Pangeran Adipati Anom (Prabu Amêngkunagara III, son of Sunuhun Pabubuwana IV) ordered the scribe (poet) Sutrasna to compose a manuscript in the form of songs (poetry) containing all aspects of Javanese Knowledge, so that it could serve as a primary reference for Javanese Wisdom. The plot of the story is about the journey of Radèn Jayèngrêsmi. The composition of the manuscript began on Saturday Paing, 26th of Sura, with the chronogram 'paksa suci sabda ji', Javanese year 1742. The manuscript was given the name 'Suluk Tambangraras'.

## II. The Chronicle of Giri
Sèh Wali Lanang, a scholar from the city of Jeddah abroad, went to Ngampèl (Gresik, East Java), then to Banyuwangi. The daughter of King Blambangan was suffering from a severe illness. Based on the advice of Patih Samboja, the King sought the help of Sèh Wali Lanang.

The princess recovered and was subsequently married to Sèh Wali Lanang. King Blambangan was asked to convert to Islam, but he refused, causing Sèh Wali Lanang to leave. Patih Samboja was saddened and then went to visit Majapahit.

The wife of Sèh Wali Lanang became pregnant and eventually gave birth to a son. The baby was wrapped in cloth and then cast away in the forest, found by a shipping merchant, and then entrusted to Nyi Samboja, a widow who had no children. The baby was adopted and given the name Santri Giri.

At age 12, he was sent to study with Sunan Ngampèl, becoming a peer to Ngampèl's son named Santri Bonang. Upon reaching adulthood, the two peers intended to go to Mecca, but in Malacca, they met Sèh Wali Lanang and were told to return. Santri Giri was given the title Prabu Sètmata and Santri Bonang was given the title Prabu Anyakrakusuma. Santri Giri eventually ascended as Sunan Giri.
